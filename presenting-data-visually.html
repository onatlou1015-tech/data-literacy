Part 1

Why Present Data Visually?

Numbers in a table are useful — but charts make patterns easy to see. A good visual shows the story of your data at a glance.

👁️ Charts Show Patterns

  • Trends and comparisons become obvious.
  • Big differences stand out instantly.
  • Audiences understand faster.

🧠 Charts Help Memory

  • We remember pictures better than numbers.
  • A clear chart is easier to share.
  • It makes your data more convincing.

💡 Key Idea

  • Presenting is the fourth step of the data cycle: Collect → Process → Evaluate → Present.
  • A good chart communicates your message without needing lots of words.
  • The wrong chart can confuse or mislead — choose carefully.
Part 2

Four Types of Charts

Each chart type is best for a different kind of data. Learning to match the chart to the data is a key data skill.

📊 Bar Chart

Uses bars to compare categories.

7
4
3
🍎 Apple 🍌 Banana 🍊 Orange
Best for: Comparing categories, like favourite sports or fruits.

🥧 Pie Chart

Shows parts of a whole as slices of a circle.

Walk Bus Car Bike
Best for: Percentages and parts of a whole, like transport types.

📈 Line Graph

Shows change over time with connected points.

MonTueWedThuFri
Best for: Trends over time, like temperature over a week.

🖼️ Pictogram

Uses small pictures to stand for a number of items.

📚 Books 📗📗📗📗📗
🎨 Art 📗📗📗
🎵 Music 📗📗📗📗
Best for: Simple counts for young audiences, like number of books read.

💡 Key Idea

  • Bar charts = compare categories.
  • Pie charts = show parts of a whole.
  • Line graphs = show change over time.
  • Pictograms = simple counting with pictures.

Part 4

Good Chart Design

The right chart type is only half the job. A chart must also be clearly designed so it doesn't confuse or mislead.

📝

Clear Title

Every chart needs a title telling what it shows.

📏

Labeled Axes

Both axes must have labels and units (e.g., °C, kg).

📐

Proper Scale

Start bar charts at 0. Keep the scale even.

🚫

No Misleading Visuals

Don't stretch, shrink, or distort to exaggerate.

💡 Key Idea

  • A clear, honest design builds trust in your data.
  • Always start bar charts at 0 — never cut the axis.
  • Labels and titles make your chart understandable on its own.
Part 5

Tools for Making Charts

You don't have to draw charts by hand. Digital tools can turn your data into professional-looking charts in seconds.

📊

Google Sheets

Free online tool. Insert charts directly from your data.

📗

Microsoft Excel

Powerful desktop tool. Wide range of chart types.

🎨

Canva

Design tool with beautiful chart templates.

🖼️

Google Slides

Add charts into your presentation slides.

📈

Infogram

Online tool for interactive infographics.

📉

Piktochart

Great for posters and visual reports.

💡 Key Idea

  • Spreadsheets like Google Sheets and Excel are the fastest way to make charts from data.
  • Design tools like Canva help you make charts look professional.
  • Always check the chart after making it — is it clear, labelled, and honest?
Activity

Let's Practise! 🎯

📝 Activity 1: Choose the Right Chart

  1. The favourite sports in your class. → Which chart type?
  2. The percentage of students who walk, cycle, or take the bus. → Which chart type?
  3. The temperature every day for one week. → Which chart type?
  4. The number of books each student in Grade 1 read. → Which chart type?

💻 Activity 2: Build a Chart in Google Sheets

  1. Enter this data in Google Sheets:
    Apple: 4 · Banana: 7 · Orange: 3
  2. Select the data, then click Insert → Chart.
  3. Choose a bar chart. Give it a clear title.
  4. Then try a pie chart with the same data. Which one tells the story better?

✍️ Activity 3: Spot the Design Problems

  1. A bar chart has no title and no axis labels. What's wrong?
  2. A bar chart starts at 80 instead of 0. What does this do to the story?
  3. A pie chart has 12 slices. Is that a good design choice? Why or why not?

💬 Class Discussion

  • Which chart type do you find easiest to read — and why?
  • Why is a bar chart usually better than a pie chart for comparing categories?
  • What would happen if you presented important data with a misleading chart?
Summary

What We Learned Today

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Charts make patterns easy to see and help others understand your data.
  • Four main charts: bar, pie, line, and pictogram.
  • Choose the chart that matches the story you want to tell.
  • Good design needs: clear title, labeled axes, proper scale, no misleading visuals.
  • Tools like Google Sheets, Excel, Canva, and Infogram help you create charts.
🎓 Student Outcome: You can now choose the right chart for your data and create it using software.
