Primary Data vs.
Secondary Data

Grade 7 · Data Detectives Unit

💬 “Who collected the data? That tells you what kind it is.”
🧑‍🔬 Primary = You
📚 Secondary = Someone else
Part 1

Two Types of Data

Not all data comes from the same place. When we ask “Who collected this data?” we can sort it into two types.

🧑‍🔬

Primary Data

Data that you collect yourself — using surveys, experiments, observations, or interviews.

📚

Secondary Data

Data that someone else collected — found in books, websites, or reports.

💡 Key Idea

  • The difference is WHO collected it, not what the data is about.
  • Both types can be useful — but you must know which one you are using.
  • Primary = first-hand. Secondary = second-hand.
Part 2

Primary Data — You Collect It Yourself

Primary data is information you gather first-hand. You are the one asking the questions, watching, measuring, or testing.

📝 Surveys & Questionnaires

  • Asking classmates about their favorite fruit.
  • Asking people how they travel to school.

🎤 Interviews

  • Talking to the school nurse about health habits.
  • Asking a coach about training routines.

👀 Observations

  • Counting how many cars pass the school gate.
  • Watching how many students use the library.

🧪 Experiments

  • Measuring how fast a plant grows each day.
  • Testing which paper towel absorbs the most water.

💡 Key Idea

  • Primary data is original — nobody else has it yet.
  • You control how, when, and where it is collected.
  • It is perfect for answering your own specific questions.
Part 3

Secondary Data — Someone Else Collected It

Secondary data is information that was already collected by someone else. You find it, read it, or download it — but you did not gather it yourself.

📖 Books & Textbooks

  • Population numbers in a geography book.
  • Historical temperature records.

🌐 Websites

  • Rainfall data from a weather agency site.
  • Sports statistics from an official league page.

📊 Reports

  • Government health reports.
  • School board achievement reports.

📰 Articles & News

  • Newspaper stories with survey results.
  • Online articles that cite research data.

💡 Key Idea

  • Secondary data is already collected — you just find and use it.
  • It saves time, but you must check if it is reliable and up to date.
  • Always give credit to the source when you use secondary data.
Part 4

Side-by-Side Comparison

Here is a full comparison of primary and secondary data. Use this table as your reference.

🔍 Feature 🧑‍🔬 Primary Data 📚 Secondary Data
Who collected it? 🧑‍🔬 You — first-hand 📚 Someone else — second-hand
Examples of method Surveys, interviews, observations, experiments Books, websites, reports, articles, databases
Example Class survey on favorite fruit Rainfall data from a weather website
Time & effort High — you do the work Low — data is ready to use
Control You decide the questions and method You use what is already there
Accuracy You know how it was collected You must check the source
Best for Answering your own question Background research & comparison

💡 Key Idea

  • Primary = you are the collector. Secondary = someone else collected it first.
  • Both types can be useful — but they serve different purposes.

Part 6

Advantages & Disadvantages

Each type of data has strengths and weaknesses. Knowing them helps you choose the right one for your task.

🧑‍🔬 Primary Data

✔ Advantages

  • You control exactly what is collected.
  • It directly answers your question.
  • You know it is accurate and up to date.
  • You can make sure it is fair and unbiased.

✘ Disadvantages

  • Takes more time and effort.
  • Requires planning and organising.
  • Your sample may be small.

📚 Secondary Data

✔ Advantages

  • Quick and easy to find.
  • Often covers large groups or long time periods.
  • Can be free and widely available.

✘ Disadvantages

  • You did not collect it — accuracy may be unknown.
  • It may not answer your specific question.
  • It could be out of date or biased.
  • You must credit the source.

💡 Key Idea

  • Primary data is more reliable for your own question, but takes more work.
  • Secondary data is faster, but you must check if it is trustworthy.
  • Good data detectives often use both — secondary for background, primary for their own question.
Part 7

Why This Unit Focuses on Primary Data

In this Data Detectives unit, you will become the data collector. That is why the focus is on primary data.

🎯 Our Focus: Primary Data

Throughout this unit you will:

  • Design your own surveys or experiments.
  • Collect data yourself using tools like Google Forms.
  • Organise and process your own data.
  • Evaluate whether your data is fair and accurate.
  • Present your findings to others.

Why? Because collecting your own data teaches you the entire data cycle — not just how to read someone else's results.

🤔 Think About It

  • If you wanted to know what students in your class eat for breakfast, would you use primary or secondary data? Why?
  • If you wanted to know the average rainfall in your city for the last 10 years, would you collect primary data? Why or why not?
  • What are the risks of using only secondary data?
Activity

Let's Practise! 🎯

📝 Activity 1: Primary or Secondary?

  1. The number of goals scored by your favorite team, found on a sports website.
  2. A survey you made asking classmates about their favorite subject.
  3. Temperature data from a school weather station you built.
  4. Population numbers from a geography textbook.
  5. Interviews you did with the school librarian.
  6. Rainfall data from a weather agency website.

🛠️ Activity 2: Decide the Best Type

  1. You want to know the most popular snack in your class. Would you use primary or secondary data? Why?
  2. You want to know how the population of your town changed over 50 years. Which type would you use? Why?
  3. You want to know if students feel safe at school. Which type is best? Explain your choice.

✍️ Activity 3: Plan a Mini-Collection

  1. Choose a question you would like to answer about your class or school.
  2. Decide: Will you collect primary or secondary data?
  3. Write one sentence explaining why that choice is best.

💬 Class Discussion

  • Why is it important to know if data is primary or secondary?
  • Could the same data be primary in one situation and secondary in another? Give an example.
  • What should you always do when you use secondary data?
Summary

What We Learned Today

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Primary data = data you collect yourself (surveys, experiments, observations, interviews).
  • Secondary data = data collected by someone else (books, websites, reports).
  • The difference is who collected it, not what it is about.
  • Each type has advantages and disadvantages.
  • This unit focuses on primary data so you can learn the entire data cycle.
🎓 Student Outcome: You can now identify primary and secondary data sources and explain their differences.
