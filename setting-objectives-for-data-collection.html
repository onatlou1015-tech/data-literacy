Setting Objectives
for Data Collection

Grade 7 · Data Detectives Unit

💬 “A good data task begins with a clear purpose.”
❓ What?
👥 Who?
🎯 Why?
Part 1

Why Do We Collect Data?

Every data task starts with a purpose. We don't collect data just for fun — we collect it to answer a question, solve a problem, or make a decision.

Think about it:

🌦️ Weather

  • Why collect temperature every day?
  • To predict tomorrow's weather and help people plan.

🏫 School

  • Why collect test scores?
  • To see who needs help and improve teaching.

⚽ Sports

  • Why collect goals and assists?
  • To choose the best players and plan strategies.

🛒 Shopping

  • Why collect sales numbers?
  • To know what to stock and what to stop selling.

💡 Key Idea

  • Data collection always begins with a purpose.
  • If you don't know why you're collecting data, you won't know what to do with it.
  • A clear purpose saves time and prevents wasted effort.
Part 2

Three Key Questions for a Clear Objective

Before collecting any data, answer these three questions. Your answers become your objective.

1
❓

What do we want to find out?

What is the question we are trying to answer?

2
👥

Who or what will we study?

Which group, place, or thing are we collecting data about?

3
🎯

What will we do with the results?

How will this data help us decide or take action?

🧪 The Objective Formula

What? + Who? + Why? → Clear Objective 🎯

💡 Key Idea

  • A strong objective answers all three questions clearly.
  • If any of the three is missing, the objective is too vague.
  • Write your objective before you start collecting data.
Part 3

Examples of Clear Objectives

Here are three strong objective examples. Notice how each one answers the three key questions.

🚶
Example 1

“To find out how many students walk to school.”

❓ What: How many walk 👥 Who: Students in our school 🎯 Why: Plan safe walking routes
🌡️
Example 2

“To measure the average temperature in our classroom over one week.”

❓ What: Average temperature 👥 Where: Our classroom 🎯 Why: Check if it's comfortable for learning
⚽
Example 3

“To discover the most popular sport in Grade 7.”

❓ What: Most popular sport 👥 Who: Grade 7 students 🎯 Why: Plan a sports day

💡 Key Idea

  • A good objective is specific — not just “to collect data about sports.”
  • It names who or what is being studied.
  • It explains how the results will be used.
Part 4

Vague vs. Clear Objectives

Compare these objectives. Which ones will actually help you collect useful data?

❌ Vague Objective

“To collect data about food.”

Why it's weak: No clear question, no group, no purpose.

✅ Clear Objective

“To find out the most popular fruit among Grade 7 students to plan a healthy snack day.”

Why it works: Specific question + group + purpose.

❌ Vague Objective

“To study temperature.”

Why it's weak: Where? When? For how long? Why?

✅ Clear Objective

“To measure the average temperature in our classroom over one week to see if it stays comfortable for learning.”

Why it works: Specific, measurable, and explains the purpose.

💡 Key Idea

  • A vague objective leads to confusing data.
  • A clear objective tells you exactly what to collect and how to use it.
Activity

Let's Practise! 🎯

📝 Activity 1: Spot the Three Questions

  1. Read this objective: “To find out how many hours Grade 7 students sleep each night, to help us understand how sleep affects test scores.”
  2. Identify the What, Who, and Why.
  3. Write your answers in your notebook.

🛠️ Activity 2: Fix the Objective

  1. Here is a vague objective: “To collect data about music.”
  2. Rewrite it to make it clear. Remember to include What, Who, and Why.
  3. Example of a strong rewrite: “To find out the most listened-to music genre among Grade 7 students, so we can choose songs for the school dance.”

✍️ Activity 3: Write Your Own Objective

  1. Think of something you are curious about at school or at home.
  2. Write a clear objective that answers the three questions.
  3. Share it with a partner and ask: Is it clear? Can we collect data for it?

💬 Class Discussion

  • Why does collecting data without a clear objective waste time?
  • Can one data collection task have more than one objective?
  • How would a clear objective help you organise your data later?
Summary

What We Learned Today

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Every data task starts with a purpose.
  • A clear objective answers three questions: What?, Who?, and Why?
  • Strong objectives are specific and explain how the results will be used.
  • Vague objectives lead to confusing data — clear objectives lead to useful information.
🎓 Student Outcome: You can now write a clear objective for a data task that answers the three key questions.
