Part 1

Why Do We Organize Data?

After collecting raw data, it is often messy and confusing. Organising data helps us see patterns, find answers, and avoid mistakes.

🔍 Makes It Easier to Understand

  • Patterns become visible.
  • Comparisons become simple.
  • Trends jump out at you.

✅ Makes It Easier to Check

  • You can spot missing values.
  • You can check for errors.
  • You can count totals quickly.

💡 Key Idea

  • Raw data is like a pile of puzzle pieces — organising is putting them together.
  • Organised data is ready to process into information.
  • Without organisation, your data has no meaning.
Part 2

Tools for Recording Data

There are three main tools we use to record data by hand. Each one is useful in different situations.

📋

Tables

Rows and columns that keep data neat and aligned.

Best for: Recording data that has labels and values — like names, scores, or categories.
✏️

Tally Charts

Counting with marks. Every 5th mark crosses the group.

Best for: Quickly counting how many times something happens as you observe it.
🔢

Frequency Tables

Show how often something occurs using numbers.

Best for: Summarising tally counts into clear numbers.

💡 Key Idea

  • Tables organise, tally charts count, and frequency tables summarise.
  • They often work together — you can tally first, then write the frequency.
Part 3

Example: Tally & Frequency Table

Imagine you surveyed your class about their favourite fruit. Here is what the recorded data looks like in a tally and frequency table.

🍎 Fruit ✏️ Tally 🔢 Frequency
Apple //// 4
Banana //// // 7
Orange /// 3

✏️ How to Read the Tally

  • Every group of 5 marks uses a slash through 4 lines.
  • 4 lines + 1 slash = 5.
  • Leftover single lines are counted individually.

🔢 How to Read the Frequency

  • Frequency = the total count for that category.
  • Apple: 4 · Banana: 7 · Orange: 3.
  • Total collected = 4 + 7 + 3 = 14 responses.

💡 Key Idea

  • Tally marks are the live count — you make them as you collect.
  • Frequency is the final number — written after you finish counting.
  • Together they make the data easy to read and compare.
Part 4

Using Spreadsheets

A spreadsheet is a digital tool for organising data — like a smart table that can also calculate for you. Popular options are Google Sheets and Microsoft Excel.

💻 Google Sheets

  • Free online tool.
  • Works in any browser.
  • Great for sharing with classmates.

📗 Microsoft Excel

  • Powerful desktop tool.
  • Lots of advanced features.
  • Common in workplaces.

🌟 Why Use Them?

  • Auto-calculate totals.
  • Sort and filter easily.
  • Make charts instantly.

Part 5

Basic Spreadsheet Skills

Three simple skills will get you started. Once you know these, you can organise almost any data.

⌨️ 1. Entering Data

  • Click a cell to select it.
  • Type your value and press Enter.
  • Each cell holds one piece of data.

🏷️ 2. Naming Columns

  • Give each column a clear heading.
  • Use row 1 for the headers.
  • Example: Fruit, Tally, Frequency.
➕

SUM

=SUM(C2:C10)
Adds up all numbers in the range.
📊

AVERAGE

=AVERAGE(C2:C10)
Finds the mean of the numbers.
🔢

COUNT

=COUNT(C2:C10)
Counts how many numbers are in the range.

💡 Key Idea

  • Formulas save you time and reduce mistakes.
  • Use SUM for totals, AVERAGE for means, and COUNT for how many.
  • Ranges like C2:C10 mean "from cell C2 down to cell C10".
Activity

Let's Practise! 🎯

📝 Activity 1: Build a Tally & Frequency Table

  1. Ask 10 classmates what their favourite drink is.
  2. Record each answer with a tally mark.
  3. Write the frequency for each drink.
  4. Total all the frequencies — do they add up to 10?

💻 Activity 2: Make It Digital

  1. Open Google Sheets or Microsoft Excel.
  2. Create three columns: Item, Tally, Frequency.
  3. Enter your data from Activity 1.
  4. Use =SUM() to total the Frequency column.
  5. Use =AVERAGE() to find the average frequency.

✍️ Activity 3: Fix the Messy Data

  1. Here is messy data: “apple, 5; banana, banana, banana, orange; apple; banana; orange; orange, apple”
  2. Rewrite it as a proper frequency table with tally marks.
  3. Check: Does your total match the original list?

💬 Class Discussion

  • Why is it important to record data while you collect it, not later?
  • What could go wrong if your tally chart has errors?
  • How does a spreadsheet make processing data easier?
Summary

What We Learned Today

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Organising data makes it easier to understand and check.
  • Three main tools: tables, tally charts, and frequency tables.
  • Spreadsheets like Google Sheets and Excel record and process data automatically.
  • Basic skills: entering data, naming columns, using formulas.
  • Key formulas: =SUM(), =AVERAGE(), =COUNT().
🎓 Student Outcome: You can now organise data in tables and enter it into a spreadsheet using basic formulas.
