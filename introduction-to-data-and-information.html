Introduction to Data
& Information

Grade 7 · Data Detectives Unit

💬 “Data tells a story. Your job is to find it, check it, and tell it clearly.”
📥 Collect
⚙️ Process
🔍 Evaluate
📢 Present
Part 1

What is Data?

Data is a collection of raw facts — numbers, words, or observations — with no meaning attached yet.

🔢 Numbers

  • 25, 30, 28, 27
  • 87, 92, 78, 85
  • 3.5, 4.2, 3.9

📝 Words & Labels

  • Apple, Banana, Orange
  • Red, Blue, Green
  • Yes, No, Maybe

👀 Observations

  • “It rained today.”
  • “20 students raised their hands.”
  • “The plant grew 2 cm.”

📋 Lists & Records

  • Names on a class roll
  • Scores in a gradebook
  • Steps counted on a watch

💡 Key Idea

  • Data is raw — it has not been organised or explained yet.
  • On its own, data does not tell us much.
  • We need to process data to turn it into something useful.
Part 2

What is Information?

Information is data that has been organised and given meaning — so we can understand it and use it.

🧠 Data + Meaning

When we sort, count, average, or explain data, it becomes information.

🎯 Useful & Clear

Information answers questions and helps us make decisions.

25, 30, 28, 27
➡️
“The average temperature this week was 27.5°C.”

💡 Key Idea

  • Information is data with context and meaning.
  • It helps us answer questions and make decisions.
  • We create information by processing raw data.
Part 3

Data vs. Information

📦 Data

  • Raw facts
  • No meaning yet
  • Just numbers, words, or observations
  • Example: 25, 30, 28, 27

💡 Information

  • Data that has been organised
  • Has meaning and context
  • Helps us understand and decide
  • Example: “The average temperature this week was 27.5°C.”

🤔 Think About It

  • Which one can you use to make a decision — data or information?
  • Can the same data become different information in different situations?
  • Why is it important to know the difference?
Part 4

Why Data Matters in Daily Life

Data is everywhere. It helps us understand the world and make better choices.

🌦️

Weather

Temperature and rainfall data help us plan our day.

🛒

Shopping

Prices and sales data help us budget and compare.

🏥

Health

Heart rate and step data help us stay fit.

🏫

School

Test scores show us where to improve.

⚽

Sports

Goals and stats help coaches make decisions.

📱

Social Media

Likes and views show what people enjoy.

💡 Key Idea

  • Data helps us understand, compare, and decide.
  • It turns everyday observations into useful knowledge.
Part 5

The Data Cycle

Every data task follows the same four steps:

1
📥

Collect

Gather raw data using surveys, observations, or experiments.

→
2
⚙️

Process

Organise, sort, count, and calculate to turn data into information.

→
3
🔍

Evaluate

Check if the data is accurate, complete, fair, and relevant.

→
4
📢

Present

Share your findings clearly using charts, tables, or reports.

💡 Key Idea

  • The data cycle is a loop — we often go back and collect more data.
  • Every step is important — skipping one weakens the result.
Activity

Let's Practise! 🎯

📝 Activity 1: Data or Information?

  1. “32, 28, 30, 35” — is this data or information? Why?
  2. “The average class score was 78%.” — data or information?
  3. Write your own example of data.
  4. Turn your data into a sentence that makes it information.

🔍 Activity 2: Spot the Data Cycle

  1. Think of a time you collected data (e.g., counting your steps).
  2. Which of the four steps did you do?
  3. Which steps did you skip? What could you improve?

💬 Class Discussion

  • Can you think of a time when data was misleading?
  • Why do we need to evaluate data before presenting it?
  • How could the data cycle help you in a school project?
Summary

What We Learned Today

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Data = raw facts (numbers, words, observations) with no meaning yet.
  • Information = data that has been organised and given meaning.
  • Data becomes information when we process it.
  • Data matters in weather, shopping, health, school, sports, and more.
  • The data cycle = Collect → Process → Evaluate → Present.
🎓 Student Outcome: You can now explain the difference between data and information, and describe the four steps of the data cycle.
