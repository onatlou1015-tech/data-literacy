Part 1

Putting It All Together

You have learned every step of the data cycle. Now it's time to bring them together into one complete project. A data project is a journey — you start with a question and end with a discovery you can share.

Every complete project follows the same six steps:

1
🎯

Set Objective

What do we want to find out? Write a clear question.

2
📥

Collect Data

Use a survey, observation, or experiment to gather primary data.

3
⚙️

Process Data

Organise, sort, count, and calculate using tables and tools.

4
🔍

Evaluate Data

Check accuracy, fairness, and relevance. Spot any problems.

5
📊

Present Data

Use charts, tables, or reports to show what you found.

6
📢

Communicate

Explain your findings clearly and share them with others.

💡 Key Idea

  • A complete data project uses every step of the data cycle.
  • Skipping a step (like evaluating) weakens your whole project.
  • The best projects are thoughtfully planned from start to finish.
Part 2

Example Project Walkthrough

Follow this simple example to see how all six steps work together.

📋 Project: "Favourite Fruit in Grade 7"
🎯

Step 1 · Set Objective

"To find out the most popular fruit among Grade 7 students to plan a healthy snack day."

📥

Step 2 · Collect Data

Made a short survey with 1 question — "Which fruit do you prefer?" — and asked 30 classmates.

⚙️

Step 3 · Process Data

Made a tally & frequency table: Apple 4 · Banana 7 · Orange 3. Total responses: 14.

🔍

Step 4 · Evaluate Data

Checked the data: all 30 students answered — fair balance. No biased questions.

📊

Step 5 · Present Data

Made a bar chart showing the three fruits. Banana was clearly the highest.

📢

Step 6 · Communicate

Presented to the class: "Banana is the most popular fruit — nearly 50%. Let's include it in our snack day!"

💡 Key Idea

  • Notice how every step leads to the next.
  • The objective shapes the method, the data shapes the presentation.
  • Ending with a conclusion and action makes the project meaningful.
Part 3

Working in Groups with Clear Roles

Data projects are often team efforts. When everyone has a role, the work goes faster and better. Here are some roles your group could use:

👑

Project Leader

Keeps the team on track and makes sure everyone does their part.

📝

Survey Designer

Writes the questions and creates the survey form.

📊

Data Organiser

Enters data into tables and spreadsheets.

🔍

Evaluator

Checks the data for accuracy, fairness, and problems.

🎨

Chart Maker

Creates charts and visuals for the presentation.

🎤

Presenter

Explains the findings to the class or audience.

💡 Key Idea

  • Clear roles prevent confusion and duplicated work.
  • Rotate roles so everyone learns every part of the process.
  • Great teams help each other — the project succeeds together.
Part 4

Reflecting on the Project

After finishing your project, take time to reflect. Looking back helps you do even better next time.

🎉 What Worked Well

  • Did our survey questions get clear answers?
  • Was our data easy to organise?
  • Did our charts communicate clearly?
  • Did we work well as a team?
  • Were we honest and fair with our data?

💡 What Could Improve

  • Could we have asked more people?
  • Were any questions biased or unclear?
  • Did we miss any important data?
  • Could our charts be clearer?
  • What would we do differently next time?

💡 Key Idea

  • Reflection is part of good data work — not an extra step.
  • Every project teaches you something for the next one.
  • Being honest about weaknesses makes you a better data detective.

Final Activity

Let's Do a Complete Project! 🏆

🎯 Activity 1: Plan Your Project

  1. Choose a topic you want to investigate in your school or class.
  2. Write a clear objective (remember: What? Who? Why?).
  3. Decide which method you will use: survey, interview, observation, or experiment.
  4. Write your 3 main questions for your data collection.

📋 Activity 2: Assign Group Roles

  1. In your group, assign these roles:
    Leader · Survey Designer · Data Organiser · Evaluator · Chart Maker · Presenter.
  2. Write your name next to your role on a piece of paper.
  3. Discuss what each person is responsible for.

🏆 Activity 3: Bring It All Together

  1. Collect your data from at least 20 people.
  2. Organise it in a tally & frequency table.
  3. Make one chart in Google Sheets or Excel.
  4. Present your findings in the 5-part structure: Objective → Method → Results → Meaning → Conclusion.

💬 Final Reflection

  • Which step of the data cycle did you enjoy the most?
  • Which step was the most challenging?
  • What is one thing you will do differently in your next data project?
  • How could data help you in other subjects or in your daily life?
Summary

What We Learned Today

📌 Key Takeaways

  • A complete data project uses six steps: Objective → Collect → Process → Evaluate → Present → Communicate.
  • Every step builds on the one before it.
  • Working in groups with clear roles makes projects stronger.
  • Reflection at the end helps you improve for next time.
  • You have completed the full data cycle — you are now a Data Detective!
🏆 Student Outcome: You can now complete a full data project from start to finish — collecting, processing, evaluating, and presenting real data.
