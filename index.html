🌟 Introduction: Become a Data Detective

Every day, you are surrounded by data. When you check the weather, count your steps, look at your test scores, or scroll through video likes — you are seeing data in action. But data on its own is just… numbers, words, or observations. It only becomes powerful when we turn it into information that tells a story.

That is exactly what this unit is about.

In the next lessons, you will learn how to collect, process, evaluate, and present data — just like real scientists, journalists, and business analysts do. You will start by understanding what data actually is and how it becomes meaningful information. Then you will learn how to ask the right questions, choose the best way to gather data, and organize it so it makes sense.

But collecting data is only the beginning. You will also discover how to process it using tables, charts, and spreadsheet tools, how to judge whether data is trustworthy and fair, and how to present your findings in a way that others can understand and believe.

Along the way, you will use digital tools like Google Sheets, Google Forms, and Canva — the same tools professionals use every day. You will also learn how to stay safe online and how to give credit to your sources.

By the end of this unit, you will complete your very own data project from start to finish: choosing a topic, collecting real data, analyzing it, and sharing what you discovered with others.

💬 “Data tells a story. Your job is to find it, check it, and tell it clearly.”

TOPIC 2

Setting Objectives for Data Collection

Every data task starts with a purpose. A clear objective answers:

  • What do we want to find out?
  • Who or what will we study?
  • What will we do with the results?
🚶 “How many students walk to school?”
🌡️ “Average classroom temperature over one week”
⚽ “Most popular sport in Grade 7”
✅ Write a clear objective before collecting data

TOPIC 3

Primary vs. Secondary Data

Primary — you collect it (survey, experiment, observation)
Secondary — someone else collected it (books, websites, reports)

Examples: Class survey = primary. Rainfall data from weather agency = secondary.

⭐ This unit focuses on primary data — you are the data detective!

TOPIC 6

Processing Data

Turning raw data into useful information: Sorting, counting, averages, percentages, grouping.

=SUM(B2:B10) → total
=AVERAGE(B2:B10) → mean
=COUNT(B2:B10) → how many

Software can do this automatically — sort, calculate, and summarise.
