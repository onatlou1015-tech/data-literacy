Part 1

What Does "Processing" Mean?

Processing means turning raw data into useful information. We take the numbers, words, and observations we collected, and we work on them — sorting, counting, calculating — until they tell us something.

📦 Raw Data
25, 30, 28, 27
➡️
⚙️ Processing
add, sort, average
➡️
💡 Information
"Average = 27.5°C"

💡 Key Idea

  • Raw data has no meaning until we process it.
  • Processing is the second step of the data cycle: Collect → Process → Evaluate → Present.
  • Processing answers questions like: What is the total? What is the average? What is most common?
Part 2

Common Processing Tasks

There are five main tasks we do when processing data. Each one reveals something different about the data.

🔄

Sorting

Arranging data in order — smallest to largest, or grouped by category.

Sort scores: 45, 67, 82, 91, 98
🔢

Counting

Finding totals and how many items are in each group.

Count: 14 responses
📊

Averages

Finding the mean, median, or mode of a set of numbers.

Mean of 4, 6, 8 = 6
%

Percentages

Finding what portion of the whole each group represents.

7 out of 14 = 50%
📂

Grouping

Putting data into categories so patterns become clear.

Group by: Fruit / Vegetable / Grain

💡 Key Idea

  • Each task reveals a different angle on the data.
  • Together they turn a pile of numbers into a clear story.
  • You don't always need all five — choose the ones that answer your objective.
Part 3

Averages: Mean, Median, Mode

When we say "average," we could mean one of three different things. Each one tells a slightly different story.

➗

Mean

Add all the numbers, then divide by how many there are.

4 + 6 + 8 = 18
18 ÷ 3 = 6
🎯

Median

The middle number when the list is sorted in order.

Sorted: 4, 6, 8
Median = 6
🔁

Mode

The number that appears most often in the list.

4, 6, 6, 8, 9
Mode = 6

💡 Key Idea

  • Mean is the "even share" — good for overall level.
  • Median is the "middle" — good when there are extreme values.
  • Mode is the "most common" — good for finding the favourite.
Part 4

Using Software to Process Data

Spreadsheets like Google Sheets and Microsoft Excel do the calculations for you. You just enter formulas — and they update automatically when the data changes.

➕

SUM

=SUM(B2:B10)
Adds all numbers in the range.
→ Total
📊

AVERAGE

=AVERAGE(B2:B10)
Finds the mean of the numbers.
→ Average
🔢

COUNT

=COUNT(B2:B10)
Counts how many numbers are there.
→ How many

  • Spreadsheets also have sorting tools and chart tools — you can process visually too.
Part 5

More Spreadsheet Tools

Spreadsheets don't just calculate — they can also sort, filter, and chart automatically.

🔄 Sorting Tools

  • Sort a column from A→Z or Z→A.
  • Sort numbers from smallest to largest.
  • Instantly rearranges the table.

🔎 Filtering

  • Show only rows that match a rule.
  • Example: only scores above 80.
  • Great for finding patterns.

📈 Charts

  • Turn data into bar, line, or pie charts.
  • Charts update when data changes.
  • Ready to share with others.

💡 Key Idea

  • Software can do the repetitive work in seconds.
  • You still decide what to calculate and what it means.
  • Processing is where raw data becomes information.
Activity

Let's Practise! 🎯

📝 Activity 1: Calculate by Hand

  1. Here are five scores: 72, 85, 90, 85, 68.
  2. Find the mean (add all, divide by 5).
  3. Find the median (sort first, then find the middle).
  4. Find the mode (which number repeats?).
  5. What percentage of the scores are 85 or above?

💻 Activity 2: Use a Spreadsheet

  1. Open Google Sheets or Excel.
  2. Enter the scores above in column B, rows 2 to 6.
  3. Use =SUM(B2:B6) to find the total.
  4. Use =AVERAGE(B2:B6) to find the mean.
  5. Use =COUNT(B2:B6) to count the scores.
  6. Change one score and see the totals update automatically.

✍️ Activity 3: Group and Compare

  1. Take the same scores: 72, 85, 90, 85, 68.
  2. Group them: Below 80 and 80 and above.
  3. Calculate the percentage in each group.
  4. Which group has more? What does that tell you?

💬 Class Discussion

  • Why is it useful to know the mean, median, and mode — not just one?
  • What could go wrong if you enter the wrong range in a formula?
  • How does processing help you evaluate data later?
Summary

What We Learned Today

📌 Key Takeaways

  • Processing = turning raw data into useful information.
  • Five common tasks: sorting, counting, averages, percentages, grouping.
  • Averages can mean mean, median, or mode — each tells a different story.
  • Spreadsheets do the work with formulas: =SUM(), =AVERAGE(), =COUNT().
  • Software also helps with sorting, filtering, and charting.
🎓 Student Outcome: You can now process data using common tasks and spreadsheet formulas and tools.
