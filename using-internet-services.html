Part 3

Safe and Responsible Use

The internet is powerful — but it also comes with responsibilities. When doing data work online, follow these four rules.

🔒

Protect Personal Data

Never share full names, addresses, or phone numbers of classmates.

🛡️

Respect Privacy

Keep survey answers anonymous. Don't reveal who said what.

🙏

Give Credit to Sources

Always say where you got secondary data — website name and link.

✅

Use Reliable Websites

Check if a source is trustworthy before using its data.

💡 Key Idea

  • Being safe online means protecting others' data as carefully as your own.
  • Good data work always involves honesty and respect.
Part 4

Evaluating Online Sources

Not every website is trustworthy. Before using data you found online, ask these three questions:

1
👤

Who made it?

"Who is the author?"

Is it a government site, university, news agency, or a random blog?

2
📅

When was it updated?

"Is it recent enough?"

Data from 2005 may no longer be true. Look for a date.

3
✅

Is it reliable?

"Can I trust it?"

Does it cite sources? Is it well-written? Do other sites agree?

✅ Usually Trustworthy

Government websites (e.g., .gov, .edu)
Museums, libraries, and universities
Well-known news agencies (BBC, Reuters)
Official statistics offices

⚠️ Question These

Personal blogs with no author info
Websites trying to sell something
Posts with no date or sources
Sites with shocking headlines

💡 Key Idea

  • Anyone can publish online — but not everything is true.
  • Use the three questions (Who? When? Trustworthy?) to check every source.
  • When in doubt, find a second source that says the same thing.
Activity

Let's Practise! 🎯

📝 Activity 1: Match the Tool to the Task

  1. You need to ask 30 classmates a question. → Which tool?
  2. You need to average 30 answers. → Which tool?
  3. You need to make a slide presentation of your findings. → Which tool?
  4. You need to share your final report with your teacher. → Which tool?

🔍 Activity 2: Evaluate a Website

  1. Find a website with a chart or survey result about a topic you like.
  2. Answer the three questions:
    Who made it? When was it updated? Is it reliable?
  3. Would you use this data in your project? Why or why not?

🔐 Activity 3: Safe Data Habits

  1. You made a Google Form asking for names and birthdays. Is this safe?
  2. Rewrite the survey so it collects the same data safely.
  3. Which of the four safety rules does your rewrite follow?

💬 Class Discussion

  • Why is it important to protect other people's data online?
  • What could happen if you used data from a fake news website?
  • How can online tools make data work faster and easier?
